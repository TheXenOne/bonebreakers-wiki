# The Barrow Arc

The Bonebreakers’ tale begins in Barrow Spa, where undead raids from the nearby Barrow have frightened travellers and strangled trade. Hired to investigate, a small band of adventurers descends into the tomb and discovers Quintius, a failed necromancer whose attempt to restore life to ancient skeletons instead unleashed them upon the countryside.

Their first expeditions end in disaster. Stan, Ike and Virien fall, Arden barely escapes with a handful of blue gems, and the survivors retreat to seek resurrection for their companions. Virien’s return gives the battered company a second chance. Joined by new allies including Ulrich, Bran “The Wolf”, Grog, Rhian and Dreg, they finally take a name: **the Bonebreakers**.

Their return to the Barrow is different. Better armed and hardened by loss, they destroy its undead, outwit its traps and uncover Quintius’ hidden treasure. They emerge not merely alive, but rich.

What began as a desperate job for a frightened town has forged an adventuring company—and given the Bonebreakers their first taste of the wealth and danger buried beneath Agria.

# The Shrine of the Oozing Serpent Arc

Fresh from their victory in the Barrow, the Bonebreakers are sent into the Kelfrek Marsh to investigate travellers vanishing along the King’s Road. The culprit is said to be a “Grease Dragon,” but the trail leads instead to the ruined **Shrine of the Oozing Serpent**, an ancient gnomish complex dedicated to the demon Mulvis.

Within its clockwork halls they discover the dragon **Sootmurk**, worshipped by strange frog-like Gloops and surrounded by fanatical servants. Their first assault ends in capture, but bluff, betrayal and fire turn the tables. Returning with the deceitful treasure-hunter Gorfthistle and a reactivated mechanical effigy of Mulvis, the Bonebreakers finally kill Sootmurk—though Barny falls in the battle and must later be restored to life.

Greed then draws them deeper. In the tomb of King Bomfluke, a new thief named Neem betrays the party, Gorfthistle dies in the ensuing bloodshed, and an exploding automaton claims Dreg. The survivors escape with Bomfluke’s crown and a growing collection of dangerous magical relics.

The expedition leaves them richer and more notorious than before. More importantly, it establishes a pattern that will follow them for years: every victory opens another sealed door.

# The Jeweller’s Sanctum Arc

The Bonebreakers’ growing reputation brings them to Tarantella Manor, where Jessum Tarantella asks them to reclaim the underground workshop of his grandfather, the jeweller-magician **Draxas Tarantella**.

Beneath the ruined estate lies the Jeweller’s Sanctum: a sprawling maze of traps, automatons, cursed jewellery and magical experiments. The Bonebreakers raid its libraries and vaults, lose Virien to a deadly ooze and restore him once again, and eventually free **Morlan**, Draxas’ long-imprisoned apprentice, from a magical mirror. Morlan proves no more trustworthy than the Sanctum itself, but survives long enough to help lead them toward its deepest chambers.

There the party destroys the Rat Queen, strips Draxas’ laboratories of their remaining treasures and escapes with an encoded gnomish volume recovered from Jessum’s inheritance.

When the book is finally deciphered, the Sanctum’s true legacy is revealed: a map to a distant island marked by a **golden Asclepieion**.

For the first time, the Bonebreakers’ next adventure lies beyond Barrowshire.

# The Fos Imeras Arc

Following the map east, the Bonebreakers reach **Ikiria**, the glittering port city of East Agria. At the Flux Academy, Professor Ulna Glitterpatch identifies their destination as **Fos Imeras**, an island once renowned for miraculous healing but silent for years. She hires them to discover what happened.

The mission quickly becomes international. A Ramen Imperial fleet intercepts them and grants permission to land only if they investigate missing Imperial personnel and recover the legendary vessel **Eleftheria**. On Fos Imeras, the Bonebreakers discover that healing has become horror. The island’s oracle, **Sebastaeia**, now practices “purification”—destroying minds and bodies in pursuit of perfect health—while harpies masquerade as physicians among the ruins.

The Bonebreakers kill Sebastaeia and break her hold over the Asclepieion, but a storm destroys their own ship and beloved horse Lucius, leaving them stranded. Pressing on, they rescue Ossilicus and other Imperial captives and eventually discover a magical rudder connected to the lost Eleftheria. The rudder presents them with a riddle—a villain’s rib, a pound of gold, something purple and something old. Solving it allows them to call forth and command the magical vessel.

Aboard the Eleftheria, they return to the Asclepieion, destroy the remaining harpies and rescue further prisoners, including **Princess Agatha of Agria** and several nobles. With the missing Imperials saved, the Ramen fleet honours its bargain and allows the Bonebreakers to leave.

They sail home as heroes, unaware that rescuing Agatha has just drawn them into the affairs of the Agrial crown.

# Church of the Crimson Flame Arc

Back in Ikiria, Princess Agatha reveals herself as heir to the Agrial throne and governor of the East Agria Company. Impressed by Fos Imeras, she recruits the Bonebreakers for missions too dangerous—and too politically sensitive—for the crown to undertake openly.

Their first target is the **Deathless Plague**, which has ravaged the Southlands while the Church of the Crimson Flame grows increasingly brutal in its attempts to contain it. The Bonebreakers assault the church’s cathedral and uncover the truth: the Crimson Bishop himself has been undead for years, while the faith that once tried to cure the afflicted has collapsed into torture, execution and fanaticism.

The campaign is bloody. Arden, Durandale, Barny and Frank all fall at different stages of the fighting, some later restored to life. Yet the Bonebreakers eventually descend beneath the altar and confront the Crimson Bishop in his crypt. Refusing his offer of alliance, they destroy him and break the supernatural power behind the plague.

Agatha rewards them with the cathedral itself.

Arden reconsecrates it to Belenus as **the Mercy**, transforming the Bonebreakers from wandering treasure-hunters into landowners, priests and political actors.

They do not yet realise that something far older lies beneath their new home.

# Return to the Barrow and the Ninja Orcs Arc

With the Mercy established, Barny’s search for forbidden magical knowledge draws the Bonebreakers back to where everything began. Beneath the Barrow they uncover Quintius’ remaining secrets, and Arden finally purges the necromantic corruption lingering within the tomb. Barny claims the site for magical research, placing another piece of Barrowshire under the party’s growing influence.

Their new power immediately attracts enemies.

Black-clad orcs raid the Mercy and kidnap four priests, demanding a vast ransom from their leader, **Gruumsh One-tusk**. The trail leads back to the Shrine of Mulvis, now occupied by a hundred-strong orc warband.

Rather than pay, the Bonebreakers infiltrate their old hunting ground. Sleep spells, traps, grease and fire turn the shrine into a killing ground. Gruumsh’s forces are devastated, the priests are freed and the warlord is forced to surrender his treasure and withdraw.

The Bonebreakers now hold the Barrow and the Mercy, have broken a major warband and enjoy the favour of the royal heir.

Their adventures are no longer merely making them rich.

They are making them powerful.

# The Sentient Weapons Arc

Returning to Ikiria, the Bonebreakers find the city gripped by magical absurdity: weapons everywhere have begun to speak. The Flux Academy traces the phenomenon to the rogue wizard **Arkeus Whizkeyrd**, whose experiments in planar magic are destabilising reality itself.

Inside Arkeus’ impossible tower, the Bonebreakers encounter living spells, sentient artefacts, magical clones and rooms warped by other planes. They eventually discover the real Arkeus suspended as a living anchor holding shut a breach into the Astral Plane.

In trying to resolve the crisis, they free him.

The resulting rupture tears Arkeus, his clone and the tower’s wandering magical consciousnesses into the Astral Plane. The Bonebreakers destroy the crystal guardians left behind and escape as a vast magical effect drains the sea around Ikiria.

The talking weapons fall silent, but the episode makes clear how much stranger the world beyond Barrowshire can become.

Then Barny begins dreaming of dying roses beneath a blood-red moon.

Outside Ikiria, a castle has appeared where no castle stood before.

# The Halls of the Blood King Arc

Beneath a crimson moon, the Bonebreakers enter a vampire domain that has manifested outside Ikiria. Within the **Halls of the Blood King**, mortals are imprisoned for food, undead nobles scheme against one another and even the manor itself seems alive.

Rather than fight the entire court at once, the Bonebreakers exploit its rivalries. They bargain with the Blood King’s daughter, assassinate one of her enemies, free prisoners and learn from the survivor Selana that the King cannot truly die while his hidden heart survives.

Their search leads through blood-filled baths, torture chambers, secret alliances and an observatory containing an immense magical model of the heavens. There they find what the manor has been built to protect: a colossal beating heart concealed within the mechanism.

The Bonebreakers destroy it.

The Blood King’s power collapses with the heart, and the party escapes with the prisoners as the manor tears itself apart behind them.

They barely have time to enjoy the victory. On the road away from Ikiria, a giant hawk plunges from the sky and carries **Tobold Took** westward.

His kidnapping will lead the Bonebreakers into a conspiracy larger than anything they have yet faced.

# The Westmarsh Invasion Arc

The search for Tobold takes the Bonebreakers west, into the ruined frontier from which the ancestors of modern Agria once fled.

At **Westfort**, they find a settlement already being dismantled from within: its granary burned, water poisoned and weapons stolen. Children have vanished from surrounding villages. Gnolls, goblins and hobgoblins once dismissed as scattered raiders are cooperating, and every clue points toward deliberate preparation for war.

The Bonebreakers trace the sabotage through local agents and monster markets until a name emerges: **the Grey Syndicate**. The organisation is supplying money, weapons and intelligence while a hobgoblin army gathers deeper in Westmarsh.

Rose’s aerial reconnaissance reveals the scale of the threat—and four hobgoblin leaders directing preparations for invasion. The Bonebreakers strike before the army can march, killing three of the four commanders and sabotaging the fortress. But the fourth, a crowned hobgoblin, escapes. With its remaining leader still alive, the army retains enough organisation to threaten Westfort. The survivor will later be identified as **Kargaz the Disciplined**.

Yet the kidnapped children remain inside enemy territory. Returning through hidden passages, the Bonebreakers penetrate the fortress and finally confront **Ash**, one of the Syndicate’s senior agents. She offers them a place in the organisation—and asks them to investigate the mysterious Necropolis on its behalf.

They refuse.

The children are rescued and Westfort survives the immediate invasion, but Ash escapes and Tobold is nowhere among the captives.

What began as a rescue mission has revealed an organised campaign to destabilise Agria—and one surviving commander capable of continuing the war.

# Into the Necropolis Arc

The Grey Syndicate’s interest in the Necropolis becomes impossible to ignore when the Bonebreakers discover something hidden beneath their own cathedral.

Below the Mercy lies the **Abyss**, an ancient opening descending into seemingly bottomless darkness. A staircase leads down through it, but not in any ordinary way: following the steps carries the Bonebreakers across an impossible threshold, emerging into a vast underground complex that should be nowhere near the cathedral at all.

They have entered the **Imperial Necropolis**, apparently the same ruin believed to lie far away in Westmarsh.

At its centre stands a great Nexus dominated by an Imperial eagle and a ring of numbered archways leading into different parts of the dead city. Through Arch VII, the Bonebreakers find an Imperial district preserved in unnatural stillness: abandoned worksites, inverted chambers and architecture twisted by ancient magic. They meet the lost scholar Cornelius Vane and recover writings on soul-binding and necrotic magic authored by a figure named **Valedictus**.

Deeper still, they kill an ancient chimera that has laired within the complex for centuries and recover a hoard of Imperial treasure.

The Necropolis is clearly more than a tomb. It is the surviving machinery of a civilisation that vanished two thousand years ago.

And somehow, beneath the Mercy, the Bonebreakers have found a way inside.

# The Grey Prince Arc

Not long after leaving the Necropolis, the Bonebreakers chance upon **Prince Arthur**, Princess Agatha’s younger brother, travelling with his entourage.

The meeting might have remained unremarkable—until the Grey Syndicate is mentioned.

Arthur immediately orders his men to kill them.

The Bonebreakers strike first, overwhelming his guards and taking the prince alive. Once Arthur is charmed and questioned, the scale of the conspiracy becomes clear. He has been dealing with the Grey Syndicate in hopes of becoming king and is travelling west to seek guidance from its mysterious leader, the **Grey Khan**. Attacks have been carried out disguised as Agatha’s forces, and Arthur expects the Syndicate to “deal with” his sister.

The party takes Arthur with them and follows his connections back toward the Spider’s Market. There they encounter Ash again. During tense negotiations over Arthur’s secretary Wendell, **Ash claims that she is the Grey Khan**, though the party remains uncertain whether she is telling the truth.

Their pursuit of the Syndicate also brings a bitter answer to an older mystery: an assassin wearing Tobold’s likeness reveals that the missing halfling died under torture without betraying the Bonebreakers.

With Arthur, witnesses and evidence in hand, the party returns to Ikiria. Commander Smith hears their case and orders the prince formally arrested for conspiracy against the crown.

The Grey Syndicate has reached the royal family.

But while the Bonebreakers have been looking west, Ikiria has begun to die.

# The Plague of Ikiria Arc

Ikiria is under quarantine, its population devastated by disease and its lower districts ruled as much by fear as by law. A self-appointed **Inquisition** burns suspected practitioners of magic and has already destroyed the Bonebreakers’ own Temple of Belenus.

Captain Vaessa Silvertread sends them into the Plats to investigate another mystery: bodies are disappearing from the city’s grave pits.

There they meet the cleric **Maren Sollow**, whose records reveal that the “plague” is really several disasters layered together. The first victims appear to have been deliberately poisoned. Overcrowding under quarantine then allowed ordinary disease to spread. Now a third horror has begun—undead are rising among the dead.

The grave pits erupt with zombies, ghouls, mummies, wraiths and ancient Imperial wights. Maren is killed in the battle, but her work survives. Following her evidence, the Bonebreakers reach the public well, confirm that it has been poisoned and magically purify the water.

At its bottom they find something impossible: ancient Westmarsh coins, deliberately placed beneath a city that did not exist when they were minted.

The Bonebreakers report everything to Silvertread. Witnesses confirm the Inquisition’s crimes, giving her the authority to suppress them, while the party urges the city to ease the overcrowding worsening the disease.

The immediate plague is broken: the poisoned water is cleansed, the undead outbreak destroyed and the Inquisition stripped of its hold over the Plats.

Yet someone engineered the original crisis—and someone has begun bringing traces of the dead Empire into modern Ikiria.

# Return to the Necropolis Arc

With Ikiria stabilised, the Bonebreakers return to the mystery beneath the Mercy.

This time the Nexus reveals just how impossible the Necropolis truly is. Through one archway, they emerge from a gatehouse and look out upon **Westmarsh itself**, confirming that the staircase beneath the Mercy somehow connects to a ruin many miles away.

Another arch leads into an ancient residential district where the Empire has never quite stopped. Zombie labourers endlessly demolish and rebuild the same streets. Children remain in long-abandoned homes and schools. Bureaucratic records have been maintained for more than two thousand years.

At the centre of this endless reconstruction is the grotesque **Bloated One**, an undead administrator still supervising a task designed never to be completed. The Bonebreakers kill it and return toward the surface.

There they discover that the Mercy has been seized by Grey Syndicate orcs.

Trapped beneath the cathedral, the party uses Barny’s crystal ball and magic to turn the ambush around, retaking the Mercy and freeing its inhabitants. Their prisoners reveal that the Syndicate ordered them simply to **delay the Bonebreakers**. They also admit that the earlier kidnapping of the Mercy’s priests had been a Grey Syndicate operation.

Research into the cathedral then uncovers another secret. The Mercy existed long before the Crimson Knights—and the church itself was deliberately built around the Abyss. Ancient worshippers did not understand the staircase, but knew there was power beneath them.

The Necropolis was never discovered beneath the Mercy.

The Mercy was built on top of it.

# The Secrets of Valedictus Arc

As the Bonebreakers prepare to delve deeper, the cost of their widening war begins to mount.

On the road from the Mercy, the veteran warrior **Bran** sacrifices himself against a pack of terrifying mantid creatures, holding them off so Arden and Michaelis can escape. Arden later creates *Bran’s Heroic Aura* in his memory.

Meanwhile, Westfort has fallen into siege. The surviving hobgoblin commander has rebuilt his forces, goblins raid the town’s supplies and siege engines batter its defences. Seeking another route through enemy territory, the Bonebreakers infiltrate the hobgoblin fortress once more. There **Luthien** is paralysed and killed by a monstrous Krell, which begins devouring her before the others can recover what remains.

Pushing onward through Westmarsh, the party discovers another entrance into the Imperial ruins. Once inside, they realise they have entered the same Necropolis reached through the Abyss beneath the Mercy.

Among its ancient military halls they encounter undead remnants of the Empire. Captain Varro reacts with terror to a single name:

**Valedictus.**

Lord Varrick finally explains why. **High Priest Valedictus** was a master of necromancy who sought to spread his dark arts across the continent and establish dominion over it. His continued power, Varrick warns, is connected to the worsening undead incursions beyond the Necropolis.

Valedictus cannot be destroyed simply by killing him. He possesses **multiple phylacteries**, and the Bonebreakers may need a mysterious object known only as **the Stylus** to stop him.

Then the party remembers a glowing skull they once found chained inside the Necropolis.

A skull they later traded to lizardfolk.

The visions and evidence point to one terrible conclusion: it was one of Valedictus’ phylacteries.

The Bonebreakers have not merely uncovered the power behind the dead city.

They may already have helped release part of it.

# The Siege of Westfort Arc

While the Bonebreakers uncover the secrets of the dead Empire, the war they failed to end earlier reaches its conclusion above.

The crowned hobgoblin who escaped their first assault is now known as **Kargaz the Disciplined**, king and commander of the remaining invasion force. With the other three leaders dead, Kargaz alone has kept the army together and turned the failed invasion into a grinding siege of Westfort.

The town is exhausted. Its walls are damaged, food supplies are under constant attack and many inhabitants blame the Bonebreakers for provoking the war. The hobgoblins even offer a year-long truce if Westfort hands the party over.

The Bonebreakers refuse.

After disrupting goblin supply routes and preparing the town for battle, they gamble everything on a counterattack. With only a small force remaining inside Westfort, the party slips behind the hobgoblin lines carrying barrels of oil.

At dawn, they set the battlefield ablaze.

As fire spreads behind the enemy, Westfort charges across the bridge. The Bonebreakers destroy the siege engines, bring down the ogres and tear through the hobgoblin line. Wistor unleashes a lightning bolt into the enemy ranks and kills **Kargaz the Disciplined**.

With their last commander dead, the invasion finally collapses.

The Siege of Westfort is broken, and the townspeople who had begun to resent the Bonebreakers welcome them back as heroes, presenting them with the key to the city.

Yet even in victory, Barny has plans of his own.

Amid the battlefield, he secretly raises Kargaz as a zombie and turns the dead king invisible.

The Grey Syndicate has lost its army.

Valedictus remains somewhere within the Necropolis.

One of his phylacteries is missing.

And the Bonebreakers themselves have become powerful enough that what they choose to do next may matter as much as whatever their enemies are planning.

# The Fog over Harrow Arc

After Westfort's victory, Barny questions the reanimated Kargaz. The dead king confirms that the Grey Syndicate supported his war and promised him control of Westfort, turning a suspected connection into direct testimony without revealing the organisation's purpose.

The Bonebreakers travel east to a recovering Ikiria. The Temple of Merciful Life has been rebuilt, but the city's royal leadership remains unstable: Princess Agatha is still missing, Commander Smith has also disappeared, and Queen Audrey II is reportedly unwell.

An East Agria Company secretary insists that Agatha is safe and delivers a letter apparently written by her. It summons the party to Harrow, a northern port reached through the fjords and canals of the Narrows. Captain Winston carries them there aboard *Agatha's Favour*, but cannot tell them where Agatha is.

At Harrow, Rickart the Reeve extracts 7,500 gold pieces merely to let the ship enter. The Bonebreakers land among pirates and crowded docks, announce themselves at the Anchor & Chain, and begin searching for Agatha without knowing whether the summons was genuine.
