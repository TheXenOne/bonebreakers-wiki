## Narrative recap

Back at [[locations/world/agria/southlands/mercy|The Mercy]], the Bonebreakers finally test the poisoned well and learn that its danger is natural rather than deliberate sabotage. That answers one local crisis, but it does not make the cathedral feel safer. Rumours of plague in Ikiria continue, Tobold is still missing, and Barny again hears Tobold's voice from somewhere below the church, warning him not to come down.

Rather than leave the warning alone, Barny uses the [[items/party-inventory/red-seers-dagger|Red Seer's Dagger]] on [[characters/active-players/callum/retainers-followers/rattius|Rattius]] to scout the hidden spiral staircase. The route begins as a descent below the church, but Rattius's view confirms that it is not behaving like normal architecture: the walls are strangely blue, and at some point he seems to be ascending while still moving away from the cathedral. When the party follows in person and crosses into the inverted space below, the whispering presence around the cathedral fades and Ulrich finds the lower area saturated with magic.

Frank discovers a polished black-basalt trapdoor in the ceiling of the upside-down chamber and pushes his head through. Beyond is an impossibly vast domed hall whose purpose and location are unknown to the party. Eight white archways surround the chamber: seven glow behind ashen curtains, while the northern arch is sealed. A giant imperial eagle statue stands ahead with dull red gemstones set into its eyes. The archways bear ancient imperial numerals older than the Ramen forms Luthien knows, running clockwise from I above the north-eastern arch to VIII above the sealed northern gate.

When Rose and Steve press the eagle's gemstone eyes, the ash-curtains vanish. Nine skeletons immediately march out from the north-eastern arch, marked I, and Ulrich destroys them with Turn Undead before the curtains fall again. Steve activates the gemstones a second time so the party can examine each opening.

Arch I is dark, with the sound of marching and a buried city street glowing faintly beyond it. Arch II shows a narrow corridor carved with reliefs that appear to record family histories. Arch III glows violet and smells of incense; its wide corridor bears a mosaic of the empire's rise, with gods descending to give fire to humanity. Arch IV has a faint green cast and smells of stagnant water, opening onto a cracked, lichen-covered hall that slopes toward a flooded fork.

Arch V is dry and dusty, carrying the smell of ancient cloth from a broad underground boulevard lined with windows and doors. Arch VI smells of old smoke and mist and shows a corridor rising gradually toward a staircase. Arch VII is scentless and flickers around what appears to be a construction or stone-cutting area. The northern Arch VIII remains sealed and offers no view at all.

The ancient imperial markings and the buried city visible through the arches lead the Bonebreakers to realise that they have somehow reached the famed [[locations/world/westmarsh/necropolis|Necropolis]], despite entering from beneath the Mercy rather than from Westmarsh. They choose the flickering [[locations/world/westmarsh/necropolis/arch-vii|Arch VII]] and step into an abandoned stonemason's camp. The plans there show only part of a larger, strangely constructed section.

Deeper inside, the party finds a lone man huddled beside a bedroll. He says he entered the Necropolis from Westmarsh six months ago, but has experienced only eleven days while lost in this section. His maps name places such as the Vault, the Collapsed Nave, the Twins, and the Chamber. He describes the ancient civilisation as master architects and administrators who were once lawful, but later turned toward chaos and the worship of chaotic gods. The Bonebreakers offer to lead him out, only for the ceiling to collapse as they begin. Steve clears the rubble, after which the man introduces himself as [[npcs/necropolis/cornelius-vane|Cornelius Vane]] and says that he originally entered through [[locations/world/westmarsh/necropolis/arch-vi|the arch marked VI]]. Cornelius remains with the party as they return through the stonemason's camp and press deeper into the section.

Moving shadows follow the group toward Cartographer's Folly. In the next room, the floor and ceiling appear reversed: an imperial eagle is set into one wall, while a stone table, chairs, cabinet, and scroll case are fixed overhead. Frank climbs to the inverted furniture, recovers another map from the scroll case, and lowers a rope so the others can follow.

The adjoining chamber is level again, with smooth walls and an iron-bound book chained to a stone plinth. Soul-binding sigils cover the book, and eight numbered iron plates around it bear compulsion runes. The door closes by itself behind the party, though Steve simply opens it again. After failing to pull the book free, Steve breaks the chains holding it shut. Barny determines that the text concerns necrotic magic, souls, and binding, then reads it as quickly as possible. He gains `+1` Intelligence and learns that the treatise was written by someone named [[npcs/necropolis/valedictus|Valedictus]]. At this point, the name means nothing more to the Bonebreakers.

Beyond lies the Foundation Chamber, apparently the lowest point in this section of the complex. Its walls are smooth through some force other than ordinary craft. A skull rests in a shallow central depression, surrounded by iron stakes and chains that have already been severed. Steve throws a rock at the skull, but it is harmlessly repelled. Frank translates the surrounding pre-imperial writing as a creation myth in which a being made the world, regretted doing so, and has sought to undo it ever since. Ulrich attempts to Turn Undead, but nothing responds. Steve's search also uncovers 200 gold pieces of pre-imperial coinage and a [[items/party-inventory/black-stone-ritual-knife|black stone ritual knife]] worth 600 gold pieces.

By the end of the session, the poisoned well has been explained, but the Mercy has become an entrance to a vast buried city whose distorted architecture, broken restraints, and surviving necromantic writings point toward dangers far older than the cathedral above.
